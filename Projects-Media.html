Shot Coach - ZerOne Hockeyology

Created a golf swing-esque motion analyzer, built specifically for hockey slap shots. I worked with professional shot coaches to design the metrics and benchmarks, and built the pipeline around the Orbbec Femto Bolt depth camera with the Azure Kinect Body Tracking SDK.

The pipeline consists of a recording system that handles the initial capture and body landmark detection, and a dockerized web application that breaks each shot into phases, scores the movement, and generates a summary PDF report.

The platform launched at the facility in April 2026 and ran 349 assessments in its first six months. I ran the first two months of sessions myself, then trained six staff to run the whole pipeline on their own.

GaMA - Gaze and Movement Assessment

Led the development of GaMA, a platform that turns raw motion capture and eye tracking recordings into analysis-ready data about how people move and where they look. Before GaMA, gaze data was labelled by hand and motion analysis was mostly limited to single joint angles.

GaMA started as part of a DARPA-funded prosthetics project at the BLINC Lab. It's since been used by research teams at the University of Alberta, the University of Auckland, and Shirley Ryan AbilityLab, has supported at least seven peer-reviewed studies, and is now the backbone of the company I co-founded.

MEng Capstone - Auto-Segmenting Motion Capture Data

Opted to tackle a neeeeedlessly large project as my MEng Capstone.

Breaking down complex, multi-action movements into discrete motions is one of the most arduous parts of analyzing motion capture data. GaMA does it with hand-built rulesets, which take expertise and a lot of iteration to get right. I wanted to see if machine learning could learn the segmentation from labelled examples instead.

Using 1,371 labelled trials collected across four sites, I benchmarked LSTMs against simpler MLP baselines in JAX. The best LSTM hit 93% frame-level accuracy, but the MLPs came surprisingly close, which suggests the problem depends far less on history than expected. Gradient-boosted trees are the natural next experiment.

Axis Reloaded

Worked with Eric Wells and Mark Sherstan to design, construct, and program a robotic kinetic sculpture as a hardware demo for the University of Alberta Faculty of Engineering.

The "claw" uses machine vision (a webcam in the palm) to track deviations in the rotating plate and trigger a re-center. The whole device is self-contained and autonomous, using a Raspberry Pi for trajectory calculation and device control. This project was additionally demoed at the January 2020 edition of Telus World of Science's Dark Matters.
